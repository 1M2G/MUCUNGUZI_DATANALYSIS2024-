### 1. Essay describing the insights derived from the article

This essay outlines the core insights derived from the endeavor to build Natural Language Processing (NLP) tools for **Runyakitara**, a group of four closely related Bantu languages spoken in Western Uganda.

The review article reveals that while Runyakitara is spoken by a sizable population, estimated at about **8 million people (13% of Uganda’s population)**, it is under threat.  
This threat stems not primarily from low speaker numbers, but from **shifting domains of use** and **diminished inter-generational transmission**. The language lacks the **social prestige** and **economic benefits** associated with Uganda’s official languages, such as *English* and *Kiswahili*.

The primary insight is the **severe lack of computational linguistic resources** for Runyakitara, distinguishing it as a **low-resource language** compared to major world languages.  
To bridge this gap and support language revitalization efforts, the project focuses on creating two fundamental resources for the **Runyankole-Rukiga** pairing:

- **RunyaCorpus** – a collection of written and spoken Runyakitara data gathered from various sources, including newspapers, public documents, the Bible, and error-annotated student essays.  
- **RunyaMorph** – a morphological analyser designed to handle the highly productive and complex morphology of Runyakitara.

The crucial technical insight is that **the corpus and the morphological analyser must be used together.** Neither resource is reliable independently.  
- The **corpus** helps address contextual issues such as phrase contraction and homographic ambiguity.  
- **RunyaMorph** provides the necessary linguistic structure to manage the vast number of possible word formations.

These foundational tools underpin the development of two key applications:

1. **RunyaSpeller** – a spell-checker designed to address common writing inaccuracies in Runyakitara, such as spelling errors and confusion between short and long vowels.  
2. **LearnRunya** – a system designed to aid revitalization by providing accessible teaching materials for both native and non-native speakers.

The use of morphological analysis has already proven effective in pilot **CALL systems** for modeling complex syntactic features like agreement and flexible word order in Bantu languages.

In a nutshell, the project serves as a **test case for utilizing computational technology** to support the **future survival and revitalization of endangered, low-resource languages** through education and increased digital prestige.

### 2. Recommendations to the ethnic leaders of the Runyakitara speakers regarding the use of NLP models for language preservation

- **Highly prioritize community-led data collection** through mobilization activities such as songs and storytelling to foster intergenerational knowledge transfer.  
- **Collaborate with AI initiatives and experts** to develop or fine-tune NLP tools tailored specifically to the Runyakitara language.  
- **Adopt small language models for efficiency**, aligning with global efforts like the UN’s *International Decade of Indigenous Languages*.  
- **Integrate speech technologies for oral traditions** to promote daily use and literacy.  
- **Address ethical and inclusivity challenges** to protect cultural knowledge from misrepresentation and ensure preservation efforts empower, rather than extract from, communities.
